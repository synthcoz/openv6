You paste a proxy into a new profile, click Check proxy, and get "Proxy check failed" or "Connection error". The same proxy works fine in another anti-detect browser. That's a strong hint: the proxy is fine, but this browser checks it in a different way.

First: where does the check run?

An anti-detect browser checks a proxy in one of two places:

  • From your computer. The app connects to the proxy from your machine and fetches an IP-check page through it. If the proxy works in a normal browser on that computer, this check passes.
  • From the vendor's servers. The app asks the vendor's cloud to test the proxy. The connection then comes from an Internet IP you don't control, not from your computer.

The second kind is the usual reason a proxy "works in one browser and not the other". A proxy that only accepts connections from your home network (LAN) will refuse the vendor's servers, and the check fails. Launching the profile may still work, because the profile itself runs on your computer.

Find the cause with the connection log

Guessing between six causes is slow. It's faster to look at what actually reached the proxy.

In openv6, open the proxy and click Check proxy in the anti-detect browser, then click Show recent connections. You see the last 60 connections to that proxy: where each came from, the protocol, the destination, how it left (IPv6, NAT64 or IPv4) and the result. Each failure type comes with a hint:

  • Refused: outside the sharing scope from an Internet IP: the browser checks from its own servers. Change the proxy's sharing to Internet (password required) if you want the check to pass, or ignore it.
  • No username/password sent or Wrong username or password: the credentials didn't import. Over HTTP, one attempt without credentials followed by one with them is normal.
  • Wrong protocol: change HTTP/SOCKS5 in the profile.
  • The destination is IPv4-only: click Allow over IPv4 on that line. See the next section first.
  • Nothing at all: the connection never reached the proxy machine. Either the address is wrong, a firewall drops it, or the check runs from the vendor's servers and can't reach your home network.

When the check service is IPv4-only

Some browsers check proxies against an IP-lookup service that has no IPv6. An IPv6-only proxy can't reach it, so the check fails even though the proxy is fine for sites that do have IPv6.

The quick fix, "turn on IPv4 fallback", has a cost: those connections leave from the proxy machine's real IPv4, the same one for every profile. For a check service that's not the end of the world, but the habit spreads to real sites. The cleaner fix:

  1. Keep IPv4 fallback off on the proxies.
  2. Add only the check service's domain to Sites allowed over IPv4.
  3. In Settings → Website access, send IPv4 through a NAT64 gateway on a VPS, so even the check service sees the VPS's IPv4, never your home IP.

The details are in why some websites won't open through an IPv6 proxy.
