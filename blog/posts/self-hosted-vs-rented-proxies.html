When renting makes sense

  • You need IPs in many specific countries or cities.
  • Your target sites are IPv4-only.
  • You need only a few proxies, for a short time.
  • You want zero setup and don't mind the monthly bill.

When self-hosting wins

  • You run many long-lived accounts on platforms that support IPv6 (Google, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and others).
  • You want static IPs that never get shared with other customers of a proxy company.
  • You already have a home connection with IPv6, a spare PC or mini PC, or a VPS.
  • You'd rather pay once than per IP every month.

The costs nobody puts in the table

  • Shared pools. Rented IPs may have been used by someone else before you, with whatever reputation they left behind. Your own IPv6 addresses start clean.
  • Your time. Self-hosting means you are the operator. Tools close most of that gap: openv6 installs as a background service, checks every proxy on a schedule, explains failures and fixes the common ones in one click, and can alert you on Telegram.
  • Network concentration. All proxies on one home connection share one /64. For accounts that must look unrelated, add more connections or a VPS with a routed /56 or /48: see /64 vs /56 vs /48.

A worked example

Say you need 50 static proxies for 50 profiles:

  • Renting static IPs: 50 × the monthly price per IP, every month.
  • Self-hosting: openv6 Pro once ($6 for one dashboard machine, $12 for three), your existing home connection and PC, optionally a small VPS for extra prefixes or a relay. The 50th proxy costs the same as the first.

If a few of your sites are IPv4-only, keep a small number of rented IPv4 proxies just for them and run everything else on IPv6.
